Why the workflow matters

What can go wrong when AI enters research?

01 / SOURCE

Source detachment

A claim may lose the source, context, or passage that should support it.

02 / STABILITY

Unstable output

The same task can produce different emphases or omissions while the prose still sounds certain.

03 / SELECTION

Hidden selection

The system may choose one source or interpretation without showing what it left out.

04 / AUTHORITY

Fluency travels

Polished language can make a weakly supported claim easier to repeat.

The workflow is protecting the route from evidence to claim. It is not trying to remove useful AI assistance.

Applied poverty, inequality, and welfare researchLanguage is not evidence by itself
AI can make research faster. It can also make an unsupported statement look ready for a paper, a brief, or a poverty-monitoring discussion before anyone has checked the source, the context, or the reasoning behind it.

The risks are straightforward. A claim can become detached from its source. A repeated generation can produce a different emphasis or omission, while the prose still sounds certain. The system can select one source or interpretation without showing what it left out. And once polished language enters a report, people may repeat it because it reads well rather than because its support is clear.

These are reasons to keep the route from evidence to claim visible, not reasons to stop using AI.

The important feature is not the names of the steps. It is that the reasoning and the checks are part of the work, rather than something reconstructed after the result is finished. Compound Research extends this structure to research questions, evidence, methods, and claims.

Commands organize the work; task types locate the workThe lifecycle connects them
The previous slide showed the workflow you already know. This slide makes the two levels of the lifecycle visible.

At the macro level, the lifecycle describes the whole research process: Scope, Evidence, Theory, Method, Execute, Verify, Communicate, and Maintain.

At the micro level, the familiar commands map onto the stages they primarily serve: Brainstorm to Scope, Plan to Method, Work to Execute, Review to Verify, and Compound to Maintain. These commands tell us how the work moves.

Task types provide a second mapping. Research Scoping belongs to Scope. Theory/Modeling belongs to Theory. Measurement and prediction belong mainly to Method. Writing and Tables/Figures belong to Communicate. Reproducibility belongs to Maintain.

Some task types span two stages, so the slide shows arrows: EDA runs from Evidence to Execute, Specification Analysis from Theory to Method, and Implementation from Method to Execute. Evidence and verification are also shared safeguards across all tasks.

Classification happens in /cr-brainstorm, where the researcher confirms or corrects the proposed type.

The point is not that each task follows a different command sequence. The same command sequence organizes every task. The task type tells the workflow which part of the lifecycle needs the most specialized guidance.

Where research responsibilities sit

The research lifecycle

01

Scope

Define the question, population, decision context, and choices that need a person.

02

Evidence

Establish the sources, data, and provenance that support important claims.

03

Theory

Explain the mechanism, model, or identification argument behind the work.

04

Method

Choose how the question will be measured, estimated, compared, or tested.

05

Execute

Carry out the planned work and record what happens, including deviations.

06

Verify

Check evidence, assumptions, diagnostics, uncertainty, and limitations.

07

Communicate

Turn reviewed findings into prose, tables, figures, or a briefing.

08

Maintain

Preserve sources, versions, code, decisions, and lessons for later recovery.

The lifecycle is the coverage: a task may focus on one stage, but responsible research keeps the other safeguards visible.

Eight responsibilities, one research processThe stages are the spine
The lifecycle is not another command sequence. It is a checklist of what responsible research needs to cover.

Scope asks what question we are answering and which choices matter. Evidence asks what supports the claims. Theory and method explain the reasoning and the way the question will be tested. Execute is the work itself. Verify checks whether the result is supported and whether its limitations are visible. Communicate turns reviewed findings into outputs. Maintain preserves the sources, decisions, and records so the work can be recovered later.

A task can focus on one stage without making the other responsibilities disappear. The task type tells us where the emphasis is; the lifecycle tells us what the whole process must protect.

Classification describes the current workIt is not a grade
These ten categories answer a different question from the lifecycle. They describe the kind of work being done, not the quality of the work and not a fixed order.

A global poverty project might include Research Scoping at the beginning, Measurement and EDA in the middle, Implementation for an estimator, Tables and Figures for results, Writing for the report, and Reproducibility for handoff. A single project can therefore contain several task types.

The classification tells the system which guidance and review surfaces are most relevant to the current task. It does not remove the shared lifecycle safeguards.

Classification is a proposal, not a verdictThe next slide shows the questions
Here is the actual task we want to show. The submitted prompt is: "/cr-brainstorm Given that people don't live independently, but they live in households, is there a more reliable way to compare living standards across countries, than using GNI per capita? Per capita assumes that people live independently. This question is more relevant because household size differs a lot across countries. The provisional classification is Research Scoping because the request is to brainstorm an approach to an open question before a measure or method has been selected. The researcher confirms or corrects that classification. A likely later task would be Measurement/Classification, but that is not the classification of this first brainstorm.

This is still the familiar command workflow. Brainstorm names the command, Research Scoping names the task, and Scope names its primary place in the lifecycle. The next slide shows what the brainstorm asks and what it might recommend.

The full wording of the research question is kept in the manuscript as the example input. This slide is an illustrative dry run, not a claim that this study has already been conducted.

What happens next

Questions first, then bounded approaches

1

What decision should this comparison inform: global monitoring, country ranking, poverty measurement, or welfare analysis?

2

What is the unit of analysis: household, person, or country average?

3

Which survey, PPP, GNI, household-size, and time-series sources are comparable?

4

What would "more reliable" mean: a better welfare concept, stable rankings, or external validity?

5

Which within-household assumptions and alternative allocation rules should be tested?

A. Equivalized survey welfare

Harmonize household consumption with PPP; compare per-capita and square-root welfare, plus one alternative. Transparent, but dependent on cross-country survey comparability.

B. Validation and sensitivity

Measure how levels and country rankings change across allocation rules, then compare them with selected external welfare indicators. More informative, but the validation target is itself a judgment.

C. Distributional comparison

Report distributions, quantiles, poverty, and inequality rather than only one country average. More honest about heterogeneity, but less simple and more data-intensive.

Working recommendation: begin with a bounded measurement study. Keep GNI per capita as a benchmark, treat the square-root rule as a candidate rather than a default, and report how levels, rankings, and distributions change across defensible alternatives.
Illustrative brainstorm outputRecommendation still needs approval
These are the follow-up questions the scoping skill is designed to surface. They clarify the decision context, the unit of analysis, the available and comparable evidence, the meaning of reliability, and the assumptions that have distributional consequences.

The brainstorm would then compare approaches rather than jumping directly to the square-root rule. The first approach is a transparent equivalence-scale comparison. The second adds sensitivity and an explicit validation discussion. The third asks whether a single average is too narrow and keeps the distribution visible.

The working recommendation is deliberately modest: run a bounded measurement study, retain GNI as a benchmark, treat square-root allocation as a candidate, and report sensitivity across alternatives. The workflow then applies its devil's-advocate check: challenge the question, simplicity, effort-value, and fit with the project focus. This is a recommendation for the next research step, not an automatic decision. The researcher still challenges it, approves or revises it, or asks for another approach before the brainstorm is saved and handed off to /cr-plan.

Command 01 / Scope

Make the research question workable

/cr-brainstorm
Clarify the question and act as a sparring partner: ask questions that flesh out ideas before choosing the method.

Leaves behind

A scoped research brief, a task type, assumptions, alternatives, and an explicit decision record where needed.

Researcher decides

What the question means, what population or comparison matters, and which value-laden choices are acceptable.

Start when the question is still fuzzyClarify before calculating
Use /cr-brainstorm when the question is still fuzzy. It begins by reading the project context, then identifies which of the ten research task types best matches the request. The researcher confirms or corrects that classification.

For research work, the command also surfaces decisions that should not be smuggled in as technical defaults. Depending on the task, that may include a sample restriction, a threshold, a weighting rule, an outlier policy, or the language used to describe who gains and who loses.

The output is not code and it is not a final method. It is a clearer question, a named task type, a scoping record, and a visible list of decisions that need to be made.

Command 02 / Method

Turn the question into a research plan

/cr-plan
Turn a clarified question into steps and checks.

Leaves behind

A plan with task type, files or data, assumptions, evidence requirements, test scenarios, and acceptance criteria.

Researcher decides

Which design is proportionate, which alternatives matter, and what counts as a credible answer.

Make the route inspectableA plan includes its checks
Use /cr-plan after the brainstorm. The plan records what will be done, in what order, and how the result will be checked. For a global poverty question, that could mean identifying the source documentation, defining the comparison, checking the relevant data, documenting a methodological choice, and stating what evidence would be enough to support the final paragraph or table.

The plan carries the task type forward. That lets later steps prepare the right evidence and review surfaces instead of treating every research request as the same kind of job.

The plan is a commitment to a method and a verification path, not a guarantee that the initial design will survive contact with the data.

Command 03 / Execute

Do the work with research-integrity gates

/cr-work
Execute the plan, one step at a time, with safeguards active.

Leaves behind

Working outputs, evidence artifacts, run records, and a report of what was completed or blocked.

Researcher decides

Whether assumptions are plausible, whether a deviation is acceptable, and whether the result answers the question.

Evidence is recorded while work happensDo not reconstruct it later
Use /cr-work to carry out the plan or a named phase. It keeps the research checks active during execution. If the work uses randomness, a seed must be recorded. If it makes a substantive claim, the source and evidence must be recorded. If it implements a derived model, the derivation and code must be compared. If the work reaches a consequential normative choice, the decision gate is revisited rather than silently defaulted.

The exact checks depend on the task type, but the principle is stable: a result should leave enough evidence for someone else to inspect how it was produced.

Command 04 / Verify

Bring the right reviewers to the result

/cr-review
Review the work through research and engineering lenses matched to the task.

Leaves behind

Prioritized findings, unresolved risks, and a decision about what must change before the result is used.

Researcher decides

How to respond to a finding and whether the remaining limitations are acceptable for the audience and purpose.

Review is matched to the claimNot a generic last read
Use /cr-review when the work needs a deliberate check. The command combines shared reviewers for code quality, testing, reproducibility, data quality, performance, architecture, documentation, and version control with research reviewers for integrity, provenance, identification, econometrics, machine learning, measurement, writing, publication output, or replication.

The task type helps determine which research reviewer is relevant. A measurement task needs attention to weighting, thresholds, clusters, and comparability. A causal design needs identification diagnostics. A writing task needs a provenance and argument check. Review is therefore not a generic last read. It is a check chosen for the kind of claim being made.

Retain the lesson

/cr-compound
Capture a verified research lesson for future work.

Leaves behind

Turn experience into institutional memoryKeep the limits attached
Use /cr-compound after a research problem has been solved and checked. It captures the reasoning, not only the final answer. The note records what was unclear, what approach worked, why it worked, and when it would fail.

That matters for applied research because a decision about a poverty line, a survey-weighted estimate, a data restriction, or a literature-search strategy can otherwise disappear into one project folder. A compound note makes the lesson available to the next researcher, with its limits attached.

How routing works

Where classification happens

/cr-brainstorm
identify the task type -> researcher confirms or corrects it

/cr-plan
record the type -> design the evidence and checks

/cr-work + /cr-review
load the relevant skills, gates, and reviewers

Classification routes the work.

Proposed early, confirmed by a personRouting is not ranking
The decision is made in /cr-brainstorm, after the project bearings are clear and before the clarifying questions and approach are finalized. The command proposes a task type from the fixed taxonomy. The researcher confirms or corrects it.

The selected type is then carried into the plan. During work, it determines which research safeguards and method guidance matter. During review, it helps select the relevant research agents. The shared evidence, normative, and integrity checks still apply across the task types.

This is routing, not automation of judgment. If a request changes, the task type can be reconsidered. If a task combines kinds of work, the plan should say which part is being addressed and what additional checks are needed.

The claim itself

Proof Carrying Claim: when does it apply?

Trigger: any substantive claim intended for reuse. It is most visible in Communicate, especially Writing, but it can originate in evidence, theory, measurement, EDA, or data work.

Literature claim

Source, version, locator, quotation, relationship between evidence and claim, and review state.

Data finding

Data source, definition, sample, method or code, run record, diagnostic, and review state.

PCC is a traceability idea, not a guarantee that the claim is true.

Do not let important claims travel aloneSupport travels with the claim
Proof Carrying Claim is not a separate task type. It is a rule that applies whenever research produces a substantive claim intended for reuse.

It is most visible in the Communicate part of the lifecycle, especially in a Writing task, because that is where a claim enters a report, paper, or briefing. But the claim record is assembled upstream, and the same rule applies to a data finding, a measurement choice, a theory statement, an EDA result, or a specification claim.

An important claim should not travel alone. It should bring the evidence and the checks that let another person inspect it.

For a literature claim about global poverty measurement, that might be the source document, its version, the page or paragraph locator, the quotation, the relationship between the quotation and the claim, and the review state. For a data finding, the corresponding support might be the data source and variable definition, the sample restriction, the specification or code, the run record, the diagnostic, and the review state.

The word proof needs a boundary here. The plugin can verify that a quotation matches a source unit or that a record satisfies an approval rule. That does not prove that the source is true, that a causal interpretation is identified, or that a policy conclusion is normatively correct.

From evidence to communication

From source to written prose

One simple trace: a sentence in a literature review points to an atomic claim, and the claim points back to a precise place in the paper.

1 / SOURCE

Long research paper

Many pages of text, tables, qualifications, and references.

Illustrative passage / paper
p. 15 / paragraph 3
extract->
2 / CLAIM

Atomic claim

One statement, kept small enough to examine and reuse.

Household size and economies of scale affect how per-capita measures represent living standards.
claim-042 / source: p. 15, paragraph 3
write from->
3 / COMPOSITION

Literature review paragraph

Because household size and economies of scale vary across countries, per-capita comparisons may not fully capture differences in living standards. [claim-042]

Trace back: claim-042
-> paper / p. 15 / paragraph 3
The point of the procedure: every important sentence can return to its claim, and every claim can return to its location in the paper.

Rafael's Data Quality Assessment work used this basic approach: break a long document into addressable text segments so a written statement can be returned to its source.

Original resources remain authoritativeA model may propose; review decides what travels
This slide is deliberately simple. Imagine a research paper with many pages of text. The procedure extracts an atomic claim from that text and preserves its location: for example, page 15, paragraph 3.

Later, when we write a literature review, a sentence or paragraph can point to the atomic claim, and the claim can point back to the exact place in the paper. The reader does not have to search the whole paper to recover the support.

This is the basic approach used by Rafael in the Data Quality Assessment work: long documents were divided into addressable text segments so drafted statements could be returned to their sources.

The plugin supports the link by keeping source segments addressable and keeping the claim record distinct from the prose. This is a slight oversimplification of the full evidence workflow, chosen to make the core idea visible.

3

5

/cr-brainstorm

Where to begin

State the research question, let the workflow propose the task type, correct it when needed, and make the decisions that matter visible before the work begins.

Thresholds, weights, samples, and framing can carry consequencesMake the choice visible
A normative decision is a choice about what should count, who is included, how burdens or benefits are described, or which comparison should anchor the argument. In applied poverty and inequality research, examples can include a threshold, a weighting rule, a sample restriction, an outlier policy, or the language used to describe distributional effects.

The workflow does not hide these choices inside a prompt or a model. The research scoping rules provide a bounded checklist for the task type. If an existing decision record covers the same study, category, context, and option, the workflow can reuse it. If it does not, the workflow stops and presents defensible options and their consequences to a human. The decision is recorded with a stable ID, its scope, the person or role who decided, the justification, and the date.

The practical starting point is /cr-brainstorm: state the research question, let the workflow identify the likely task type, correct it if needed, and make the consequential choices visible before the work begins.
