# Requirement Elicitation

## Questioning Framework

Ask questions **one at a time**, adapting based on responses. Follow this sequence:

### 1. Purpose & Problem
- What problem does this solve?
- What happens today without this? (pain point)
- What does success look like?

### 2. Users & Stakeholders
- Who will use this directly?
- Who consumes the output? (team, management, external)
- Are there different user types with different needs?

### 3. Inputs & Data
- What data goes in? Format, source, size?
- How often does the data change?
- Are there data quality concerns?
- Are there access restrictions or permissions needed?

### 4. Outputs & Deliverables
- What comes out? (report, dashboard, API, package, dataset)
- What format? (PDF, HTML, CSV, interactive)
- How often is the output needed? (one-time, recurring, real-time)

### 5. Constraints
- Performance requirements? (how fast, how much data)
- Dependencies on existing code, tools, or systems?
- Language preference? (R, Python, both)
- Timeline? (quick prototype vs. production-ready)

### 6. Edge Cases & Risks
- What could go wrong?
- What are the boundary conditions?
- What is explicitly out of scope?
- Are there security or privacy concerns?

## Tips

- Start with open-ended questions, then narrow down
- Don't ask more than one question at a time
- Summarize what you've heard before moving to the next area
- If the user says "I don't know," propose reasonable defaults
- Stop asking when you have enough to propose approaches (usually 3-6 questions)
