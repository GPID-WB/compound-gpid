# Normative Decision Register

This register contains unresolved value-laden choices for the presentation.
No option is selected by default. A choice must be confirmed by the human
owner before the corresponding narrative is treated as approved.

## ND-ai-knowledge-work-presentation-001 -- Framing language for harms and responsibility

- study: ai-knowledge-work-presentation
- plan: none (brainstorm)
- applies_to:
  - c-research/scoping/ai-knowledge-work-presentation.md
  - .cg-docs/brainstorms/2026-08-13-ai-knowledge-work-presentation.md
  - .cg-docs/plans/2026-08-13-ai-knowledge-work-presentation-production.md
  - c-research/manuscripts/2026-08-13-ai-knowledge-work-presentation.md
  - presentation opening, epistemological-threats section, and conclusion
- category: framing language for winners, losers, and burden allocation
- choice: How should the presentation describe the risk created by LLMs and assign responsibility for managing it? This includes whether stochastic generation is presented as a direct epistemic threat, a shared workflow risk, or a condition whose consequences depend on controls around the model.
- defensible_options:
  - "Strong risk framing: LLMs create a direct threat to trustworthy knowledge because fluent output can detach claims from evidence. Responsibility falls on institutions and system designers to build verification into the workflow."
  - "Balanced change framing: LLMs change both the opportunities and risks of knowledge work. Responsibility is shared by system builders, researchers, reviewers, and institutions."
  - "Workflow-dependent framing: LLMs are neither inherently trustworthy nor untrustworthy; the consequences depend on the surrounding research process. Responsibility rests primarily with the workflow and its users."
- consequences: Strong framing creates urgency but may sound anti-technology or overstate what the evidence establishes. Balanced framing is easier to share across audiences but may weaken the problem's force. Workflow-dependent framing is precise but may understate institutional responsibility.
- decided_by: presentation owner
- decision: Strong risk framing. LLMs create a direct threat to trustworthy knowledge because fluent and stochastic output can detach claims from evidence or make one unstable formulation appear authoritative. Responsibility falls on institutions, researchers, and reviewers to build systems and practices that protect against these risks while enabling useful applications.
- justification: The presentation should create urgency without treating AI use as inherently illegitimate. The risk belongs to the conditions under which knowledge is produced, and the responsible response is institutional and workflow-level protection rather than simple prohibition.
- decided_on: 2026-08-13

## ND-ai-knowledge-work-presentation-002 -- Foregrounding uncertainty and caveats

- study: ai-knowledge-work-presentation
- plan: none (brainstorm)
- applies_to:
  - c-research/scoping/ai-knowledge-work-presentation.md
  - .cg-docs/brainstorms/2026-08-13-ai-knowledge-work-presentation.md
  - .cg-docs/plans/2026-08-13-ai-knowledge-work-presentation-production.md
  - c-research/manuscripts/2026-08-13-ai-knowledge-work-presentation.md
  - slide narrative, technical lineage, and emailed version
- category: uncertainty caveats foregrounded versus deferred
- choice: How prominently should the presentation display limits on verification, lineage, model performance, generation repeatability, and the current implementation?
- defensible_options:
  - "Caveats throughout: put a short limitation beside each major claim and repeat the distinction between source-link verification and truth."
  - "Caveat checkpoint: use a clear central slide that defines what the framework can and cannot establish, then keep the main story flowing."
  - "Dual-channel disclosure: keep the talk visually spare, but include full caveats and source-status notes in the emailed narrative and speaker notes."
- consequences: Caveats throughout maximize epistemic honesty but can interrupt momentum. A checkpoint preserves a stronger spoken arc but risks later overinterpretation. Dual-channel disclosure favors presentation flow but assumes the email or notes will actually be read.
- decided_by: presentation owner
- decision: Use one central caveat checkpoint that states what the framework can and cannot establish, then let the main story proceed with that boundary in view.
- justification: A single explicit checkpoint protects the narrative's momentum while preventing source-link verification, reproducibility controls, or human approval from being mistaken for proof of truth, causal validity, or normative correctness.
- decided_on: 2026-08-13

## ND-ai-knowledge-work-presentation-003 -- Comparison baseline and meaning of progress

- study: ai-knowledge-work-presentation
- plan: none (brainstorm)
- applies_to:
  - c-research/scoping/ai-knowledge-work-presentation.md
  - .cg-docs/brainstorms/2026-08-13-ai-knowledge-work-presentation.md
  - .cg-docs/plans/2026-08-13-ai-knowledge-work-presentation-production.md
  - c-research/manuscripts/2026-08-13-ai-knowledge-work-presentation.md
  - technical lineage and future-directions section
- category: comparison baseline chosen for narrative interpretation
- choice: What should count as the main comparison when explaining the evolution and the next step?
- defensible_options:
  - "Lineage baseline: compare the original Compound Research/plugin workflow, AI-DQSS, and the current package as three successive design surfaces."
  - "Research-practice baseline: compare the workflow with ordinary literature review and answer-first LLM use, then use the lineage as supporting evidence."
  - "Two-baseline structure: use ordinary research practice to motivate the problem and the three-part lineage to explain the technical evolution."
- consequences: The lineage baseline gives credit and clarity to collaborators but may feel inward-looking. The research-practice baseline makes the presentation more useful to a broader research audience but can flatten the actual development history. Two baselines are most complete but require more time and tighter transitions.
- decided_by: presentation owner
- decision: Use ordinary research practice and answer-first LLM use as the main comparison baseline, then explain the original plugin approach, AI-DQSS, and the current package as the technical lineage that responds to the problem.
- justification: This gives both audiences a shared starting point in research work they recognize while preserving a dedicated section for the contribution and lineage of each system.
- decided_on: 2026-08-13
